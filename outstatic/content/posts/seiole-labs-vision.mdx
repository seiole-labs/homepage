\
Energy will be one of the biggest bottlenecks of our lifetime for human progress in AI and space exploration.

We have enough energy on Earth. But, the infrastructure supporting energy is obsolete: most of it was designed and built in the 1960s and 1970s. According to the White House, over 70% of the U.S. electrical grid is more than 50 years old. Imagine using a personal computer from the 1960s.

Except you cannot. Personal computers didn’t exist yet.

That's how old is the system powering your lives. Powering your hospitals, bank servers, telecommunications networks, and supposedly the entire world; including the screen you are reading this article on. It still works, but this inefficiency is losing us a lot of money and much more.

Today’s power systems are fragile, slow to adapt and not as flexible to load as we want. They were designed around more centralized, predictable assets and conservative operational assumptions. That world is changing: EVs, renewable fleets, distributed energy resources (DERs), and post‑2020 load spikes are reshaping demand. Upgrading grids isn't an easy task; the grid is the biggest machine in the world, spanning millions of kilometers. It must match supply and demand in real time while respecting hardware limits across hundreds of thousands of nodes with different constraints. That requires solving complex non linear, non convex equations in real-time also called the AC optimal power flow problem in power systems. This matter of discussion is not just about energy accessibility.

*Why this matters now*

Electricity is becoming the backbone of everything. According to the IEA projection, by 2050, electricity will **directly** supply nearly half of global final energy output; replacing core dependence on fossil fuels. Today, fossil fuel trade sits at the heart of many geopolitical tensions. In the coming decades, we can predict that countries will have to invest heavily upgrading and securing this critical infrastructure that powers their economies. That trend can be already seen. As transport, hyperscalers, industrial and manufacturing sectors are electrifying; underlying grid, market design, and optimization software needs to catch up.

This is no longer just an economic issue anymore; rather a planetary constraint. Renewable fleets cannot be simply connected to the current grids which accounts for unimaginable power wastage every year. Meanwhile, 2024 was the warmest year since records began, with global temperatures about 1.5–1.6 °C above pre‑industrial levels, signalling we are entering a 1.5 °C world.

*Our hypothesis*

We need to upgrade our grid architecture, planning tools, and market software that safely integrate large shares of renewables, EVs, DERs, and AI‑based loads optimizing cost, reliability, and emissions. Today, we are decades behind what’s possible with modern ML and computing. Integrating modern ML is tough because power systems are safety-critical, highly regulated, data-siloed, exposed to cyber risk, and must remain physically feasible even when forecasts are wrong. Interoperability, privacy, scalability, robustness, and trustworthy validation remain major obstacles. But the possibilities are enormous.

When marginal electricity cost drops and reliability rises whole new classes of activity become viable: economy driven by renewables, energy‑intensive AI research, high‑reliability energy systems where generation, storage, and conversion are tightly coordinated under extreme constraints enabling advanced space infrastructure.

In that future, it becomes possible to run high‑fidelity physics simulations in real time for global weather modeling and disaster prediction weeks in advance, to model atomic-scale interactions at scale, and to run massive foundation models locally. Frontier AI systems could become self‑improving, helping discover new laws of physics and mathematics that humans have not yet conceptualized.

That expands human consciousness by unlocking what we can build, where we can live, and how far we can go.

Seiole Labs aims to work at the core of that gap, starting with ACOPF.
